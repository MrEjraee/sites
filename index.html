AI-Built Roles

Jobs that barely existed ten years ago — created directly by the rise of AI.

⚙️ £55k – £110k
Machine Learning Engineer

Works in the technology industry building and training AI systems that learn from data. Needs a love of maths and problem-solving, plus coding skills. Usually needs a university degree in computer science or mathematics.

✍️ £38k – £75k
Prompt Engineer

Found in technology and media, this role is about crafting the right instructions to get the best results from AI tools. Needs strong communication, creativity, and clear thinking — no specialist coding required. Backgrounds in English, computing, or any creative subject can lead here.

⚖️ £50k – £95k
AI Ethics Officer

Works across government, law, and large tech companies to make sure AI is being used fairly and safely. Needs critical thinking, research, and writing skills. A degree in law, philosophy, or computer science is the typical route.

🛡️ £60k – £130k
AI Safety Engineer

Works at research labs and government organisations to make sure powerful AI systems behave safely. Needs exceptional problem-solving and logical thinking. A degree in computer science or maths is expected.

💻

Tech & Data

Technical roles where AI has become an everyday tool.

📊 £45k – £90k
Data Scientist

In demand across finance, healthcare, sport, and retail — uses AI to find patterns in large datasets and help organisations make better decisions. Needs numeracy, problem-solving, and the ability to explain results clearly. A degree in maths, statistics, or computer science is the common route.

🔐 £40k – £85k
Cybersecurity Analyst

Protects organisations across banking, government, and healthcare from hackers — AI is used on both sides of this battle. Needs logical thinking, attention to detail, and curiosity about how systems work. A degree in computer science is a common starting point.

🗂️ £50k – £100k
AI Product Manager

Decides what an AI product should do and who it is for, working in the technology and software industry. Needs communication, organisation, and empathy — understanding what users actually need. A background in business, computer science, or design all work well.

🤖 £45k – £95k
Robotics Engineer

Builds the machines that AI controls, across manufacturing, healthcare, and space. Needs strong maths, engineering, and problem-solving skills. A degree in mechanical or electrical engineering, or computer science, is the normal starting point.

🎨

Creative

Creative industries transformed by generative AI tools.

🎨 £35k – £70k
Generative AI Designer

Uses AI tools to produce images, video, and animation for clients in the creative, advertising, and media industries. Needs visual creativity, a strong eye for design, and the ability to direct AI outputs to match a brief. A portfolio of creative work matters as much as qualifications.

🎮 £30k – £80k
Game Developer

Works in the games and entertainment industry building interactive worlds — AI now creates character behaviour, generates environments, and speeds up production. Needs programming skills, creative problem-solving, and a passion for games. A degree in computer science or a strong portfolio of made games opens doors.

📰 £28k – £65k
AI Content Producer

Works in media, publishing, and marketing using AI to research, draft, and reach audiences — but always applying their own judgement. Needs strong writing, fact-checking, and storytelling skills. A degree in journalism or English is common.

🔬

Science & Health

Scientific and medical careers where AI is making a real difference.

🏥 £60k – £150k
AI-Assisted Doctor

Works in healthcare and medicine alongside AI tools that help spot diseases earlier. Still needs full medical training, plus the ability to understand AI well enough to question it and explain it to patients. Strong science subjects lead to a medical degree.

🌍 £35k – £75k
Climate Scientist

Uses AI to model weather, predict floods, and track environmental change, working in universities, government, and environmental organisations. Needs strong maths, data analysis, and scientific thinking. A degree in geography, physics, or environmental science is the starting point.

💼

Business & Law

Professional careers where AI is handling more of the heavy lifting.

📜 £45k – £100k
Legal Tech Specialist

Works in the legal industry where AI searches documents and drafts contracts — bridges the gap between law and technology. Needs reading, writing, analytical thinking, and attention to detail. A degree in law or a combined law and computing degree is the typical path.

📈 £35k – £90k
Financial Analyst

Works in the finance industry alongside AI that detects fraud and predicts market trends. Interprets what the AI finds and advises on decisions. Needs strong numeracy, commercial awareness, and clear communication. A degree in finance, economics, or maths is the most common route.

📚 £32k – £70k
EdTech Developer

Builds AI-powered tools for the education and technology sectors — products that personalise learning and help teachers. Needs empathy, programming skills, and a genuine enthusiasm for education. Degrees in computer science, education, or psychology all fit well.
